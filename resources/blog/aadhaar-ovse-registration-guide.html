UIDAI OVSE Registration: What It Is and Why Hypersign Now Holds One

Offline Aadhaar verification and OVSE registration get used as if they're the same thing. They're not. Here's what a UIDAI OVSE actually is, how registration works, and why Hypersign now holds it directly (Registration No. AF26213686).

Vikram Anand Bhushan, Co-founder & CPO, Hypersign·October 9, 2026·7 min read

Search for how to verify Aadhaar offline and the results blur together two different things. One is a framework, UIDAI's rule that a digitally signed Aadhaar QR code or XML file can be validated without a live call to the Central Identity Data Repository. The other is a registration, the specific status UIDAI grants an organization that lets it actually perform that validation under its own name. A business can operate entirely within the first without ever holding the second, routing its offline checks through whichever partner already has it. That distinction, not just a vague reference to "Aadhaar's offline framework," is what actually separates one vendor's offline Aadhaar verification claim from another's.

Hypersign now holds that registration directly. UIDAI approved Hypersign as a registered Offline Verification-Seeking Entity (OVSE), Registration No. AF26213686, moving offline Aadhaar verification from something processed under the general Offline Paperless KYC framework to something Hypersign performs under its own UIDAI registration. This post explains what an OVSE actually is, how the registration works, and where that leaves a business deciding whether to register itself or verify through a provider that already has.

What a UIDAI OVSE Actually Is

An Offline Verification-Seeking Entity is an organization UIDAI has registered to verify an Aadhaar number holder's identity by validating the digital signature embedded in a secure QR code or an encrypted XML file, without connecting to UIDAI's database in real time. UIDAI's own OVSE page describes this as a formal registration path for any entity with a lawful purpose for offline verification, distinct from the AUA/KUA licence that OTP-based e-KYC requires. The legal basis for the framework itself sits in the Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Regulations, 2021, which defines offline verification as a separate, lower-friction category from online authentication.

UIDAI's published use cases for OVSE registration span hospitality and tourism, healthcare, the gig economy, entertainment and public events, real estate and housing, education, recruitment, facility management, NGOs and trusts, government departments, and fintech, a wide enough list that "we don't do financial KYC" is not a reason to skip understanding it. The OVSE ecosystem FAQ lists the core benefits UIDAI itself advertises for the registration: the option to verify complete or selective Aadhaar data, integration directly with the Aadhaar application, reduced operational cost compared to live authentication, offline verification with a savable, auditable output, proof-of-presence through offline face verification, and a materially lower compliance burden than holding a full AUA/KUA licence. Verification only proceeds with the Aadhaar holder's consent in every case, offline or online.

How OVSE Registration Actually Works

The process UIDAI runs for OVSE registration is a direct application and technical onboarding, not a procurement cycle. UIDAI's OVSE registration page lays out the core steps: an organization submits the OVSE application with its organizational and technical details, an authorized signatory signs the application and UIDAI's terms and conditions, and the signed documents go to UIDAI for review. On the technical side, an applicant needs a callback URL where UIDAI can deliver verification responses, a public certificate valid for at least two years to secure communication with UIDAI's systems, and an organization logo in SVG format for the Aadhaar application's display. UIDAI reviews the submission, confirms details with the applicant, and completes onboarding once everything checks out.

UIDAI does not publish a fixed turnaround time for this review, and the honest answer is that the timeline depends far more on how ready an applicant's own documentation and technical integration are than on anything UIDAI controls. A registration fee applies, though UIDAI has not published a fixed public figure for it on the pages this post cites, so confirm the current amount directly with UIDAI's OVSE team rather than from any third-party estimate, this one included.

Which Path Should You Actually Take

Registering as your own OVSE makes sense when offline Aadhaar verification is a durable, central part of what your product does, and you are prepared to own the certificate renewals, callback infrastructure, and UIDAI relationship that come with it indefinitely. For most businesses building a product around identity verification rather than around being an identity verification registrant, that overhead buys very little, the actual deliverable, a verified Aadhaar record, looks identical either way to the end user.

Providers who process offline checks under the general framework rather than their own direct registration are not doing anything improper, this is a legitimate and common model, and vendors like Decentro make a fair point that integrating via an already-registered party is usually the faster, lower-risk route for a business that doesn't want to run its own UIDAI registration. The one thing worth confirming before you pick a vendor on that basis is which entity in the chain actually holds the OVSE registration, since that is a checkable, public fact rather than a marketing claim, not a reason to assume every "offline Aadhaar verification" vendor is in the same position.

FAQ

What does OVSE stand for, and what does it let an entity do?

OVSE stands for Offline Verification-Seeking Entity, UIDAI's registration for organizations that verify Aadhaar identity by validating the digital signature on a secure QR code or XML file offline, without a live call to UIDAI's database. It is separate from the AUA/KUA licence that OTP-based online e-KYC requires.

Do I need to become an OVSE myself to offer offline Aadhaar verification?

No. You can integrate with a provider that already holds OVSE registration, which is the faster path for most businesses, or register directly with UIDAI if offline verification is a long-term, core part of your product and you want to own the technical integration and compliance relationship yourself.

Is Hypersign a registered UIDAI OVSE?

Yes. UIDAI has approved Hypersign as a registered Offline Verification-Seeking Entity, Registration No. AF26213686. Offline Aadhaar QR/XML verification on Hypersign's Aadhaar Verification API now runs under this registration directly, rather than solely under the general Offline Paperless KYC framework.

Does OVSE registration replace the need for an AUA/KUA licence?

No, they cover different authentication modes entirely. AUA/KUA licensing governs live, online Aadhaar authentication such as OTP-based e-KYC, while OVSE registration governs offline verification of the Aadhaar QR code or XML. An organization can hold one, both, or neither, depending on which verification modes it actually performs.

Does offline Aadhaar verification still require the Aadhaar holder's consent?

Yes. UIDAI's offline verification framework requires explicit consent from the Aadhaar number holder before any QR or XML validation takes place, the same consent requirement that applies to online Aadhaar authentication, regardless of whether the verifying entity is a direct OVSE registrant or operating through a partner.
