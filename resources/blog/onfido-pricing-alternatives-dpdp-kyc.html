Onfido Pricing & Alternatives for DPDP-Regulated Fintechs (2026)

Onfido pricing isn't published anywhere, not even a starting number. Here's what Capterra, GetApp, and Onfido's own site (now Entrust IDV) actually confirm, why the per-verification figures floating around online don't agree with each other, and how Hypersign's published rate compares.

Hypersign Compliance Team·August 28, 2026·7 min read

Search "onfido pricing" and the results split into two camps: aggregator sites guessing at a per-check rate, and a handful that admit there isn't one to find. Both Capterra and GetApp, two of the largest software review directories, list Onfido's starting price as "Contact vendor" and "No pricing info" respectively. Unlike Sumsub, which publishes real numbers for two of its three tiers, or HyperVerge, which at least names three tiers with feature lists attached, Onfido (now sold as Entrust IDV after Entrust's 2024 acquisition) has no public pricing page at all.

This post lays out what's actually confirmed about Onfido's pricing process and product, what the acquisition changed, and why the wall of contradictory third-party estimates you'll find elsewhere shouldn't be treated as a real number.

What Onfido Pricing Actually Publishes: Nothing

There is no entrust.com/pricing or onfido.com/pricing page in any search index, and onfido.com itself now 301-redirects entirely to entrust.com/products/identity-verification, confirming Onfido no longer maintains an independent site. Capterra's listing states plainly under "Starting Price": "Contact vendor for pricing," with no free trial and no free version offered. GetApp's comparison table shows the same field as "No pricing info." Every deal runs through a sales conversation and an ROI calculator rather than a checkout.

The Onfido Cost Estimates Online Don't Agree With Each Other

Search further and dollar figures do appear, just not from Onfido. Third-party estimate and directory sites put Onfido's per-verification cost anywhere from roughly $0.80 with a $49 monthly minimum, to $2 to $3 per check, to a claimed median annual spend near $60,000 with a stated range running from a few thousand dollars a year to nearly a million. None of these numbers appear on Onfido's or Entrust's own site, none cite a primary source, and they disagree with each other by roughly four to six times on a per-check basis alone. That spread is itself the finding: treat every one of these figures as unconfirmed rather than picking whichever sounds most plausible.

One third-party analysis of Onfido's pricing structure, published by a competing vendor, names the factors it says shape a quote: verification volume, the mix of document versus biometric versus data checks, geographic coverage, feature selection, and integration complexity, the same volume-and-module drivers that push HyperVerge's own pricing behind a sales call. Read as a description of Onfido's buying process rather than a source of numbers, it lines up with what Capterra and GetApp show: a quote, not a rate card.

Onfido's Real Coverage and Features

None of this is a claim that Onfido is a weak product. Its document verification covers 2,500+ document types across 195+ countries, a filterable-by-region catalogue few vendors match. Biometric verification combines active and passive liveness detection with device and behavioral signals, and its watchlist screening covers sanctions, PEP, and adverse media with optional ongoing monitoring, built to 6AMLD requirements. Onfido carries SOC 2 Type 2 certification and ETSI-certified IDV standing for EU eIDAS-aligned verification, both independently audited credentials rather than marketing claims.

Founded in 2012 and headquartered in London, Onfido had built a base of 1,200-plus business customers before the acquisition, spanning financial institutions, e-commerce, and gaming. That's an established, credible vendor by any measure, and being folded into Entrust's wider security business (PKI, certificates, workforce identity) gives it a real cross-sell advantage for enterprises already buying other Entrust infrastructure. Hypersign's own best KYC providers roundup makes the same point: Entrust is "a solid enterprise option if you're already buying other security infrastructure from Entrust," it just doesn't publish a number to compare against anything else.

The Entrust Acquisition and What "Onfido Is Now Entrust IDV" Means

Entrust announced its plan to acquire Onfido on February 7, 2024, and the deal closed later that year. Entrust's own page on the transition is titled "Onfido Is Now Entrust IDV," and the product now sits inside Entrust's broader identity-and-security portfolio alongside its PKI, certificate, and workforce-identity lines. For a buyer, the practical effect is that Onfido's identity verification pricing is no longer a standalone conversation, it's folded into whatever enterprise sales process Entrust already runs for its other security products, which is one reason a dedicated rate card was never likely to reappear post-acquisition.

Onfido Alternatives for DPDP-Regulated Fintechs

A DPDP-regulated Indian fintech evaluating Onfido is usually not questioning its coverage or fraud detection, both are real strengths. The gap is procurement: no number exists to model against a budget until a sales cycle produces one, and nothing in Onfido's public material speaks to DPDP-specific consent requirements the way a vendor built around India's Act would.
