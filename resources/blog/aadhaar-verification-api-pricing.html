Aadhaar Verification API Pricing: What It Actually Costs in 2026

Most Aadhaar verification API vendors send you to a demo call instead of a price. Here's what five providers actually charge in 2026, including one with a public rate card, a live calculator, and DPDP-grade consent and data minimisation included in the cost.

Aadhaar Verification API Cost: Why Most Vendors Won't Tell You

If you have shopped for an Aadhaar verification API before, you already know the pattern. You land on a features page, scroll to the bottom, and find a "Contact Sales" button where the pricing table should be.

Why Enterprise Pricing Hides the Real Cost

Most Aadhaar verification vendors sell into banks, NBFCs, and large fintechs first. Those deals get negotiated case by case, based on volume commitments, SLA terms, and sometimes bundled add-ons like AML screening.

Publishing a flat rate card would undercut those custom deals, so a lot of vendors simply do not publish one. That works fine for enterprise buyers with a procurement team. It is a lot less useful if you are a smaller team trying to estimate cost before you even build.

What Actually Drives the Price Per Verification

Aadhaar verification is not one API call, it is usually a sequence. OTP-based eKYC needs an OTP generation step and a separate OTP verification step. Offline QR or XML verification skips OTP entirely and reads a signed document instead. Face match, when you need it, is billed as its own step on top of either path.

That is why "how much does an Aadhaar verification API cost" rarely has a single answer. The real number depends on which combination of steps your flow actually uses. A lending app that only needs OTP eKYC pays for two steps, OTP generation and OTP verification. A gig platform verifying seasonal workers from a physical card might skip OTP entirely and pay for a single offline QR check instead. Add face match to either path, and you are paying for a third step on top.

How to Estimate Your Cost Before You Commit

Before you sign anything, work out your expected monthly volume and which steps your flow actually needs. A team onboarding 400 users a month with OTP eKYC and face match is a very different cost profile than one running 5,000 offline QR checks with no face match at all.

Providers with a public rate card, like Hypersign, let you plug that volume into a calculator and see the number immediately. Providers without one will ask you to describe your flow on a call first, then come back with a quote days later. If you are still early and your volume is uncertain, a public calculator saves you that back and forth entirely.

Aadhaar Verification API Pricing and DPDP Compliance

India's DPDP Act requires more than a consent checkbox. It expects purpose-based, granular consent, a real audit trail, and data minimisation, sharing only what a specific check actually needs, not a full demographic dump every time.

None of that comes free with a raw Aadhaar OTP or QR API call. Someone still has to capture consent per purpose, log it in a way that holds up to an audit, and build a way to request less data instead of everything UIDAI returns. That work either comes bundled with your Aadhaar verification API, or it becomes a separate project.

Hypersign builds it in. Every verification captures consent with a tamper-evident, cryptographically signed audit trail, at no separate charge. Aadhaar numbers stay masked to the last four digits automatically. Selective disclosure through zero-knowledge proofs lets you ask for a single claim, like "age over 18," without ever receiving or storing the underlying date of birth or Aadhaar number, so data minimisation is a property of the verification itself, not a policy you have to enforce downstream.

HyperVerge and Signzy are both strong on the verification side, but their public materials do not list a consent-management or data-minimisation product alongside the Aadhaar API itself (see our Hypersign vs HyperVerge and Hypersign vs Signzy comparisons). That likely means DPDP-grade consent handling gets bolted on separately, either through a third-party consent management platform integrated into the flow, or built and maintained in-house. Setu and Surepass do not publish a dedicated consent or data-minimisation layer either, so confirm directly with each before assuming it is included.

When you compare Aadhaar verification API cost across vendors, the per-verification rupee figure is only part of the number. What it costs to reach DPDP-grade consent and data minimisation on top, whether that is a subscription to a separate consent platform, engineering time to build it, or a compliance gap you carry until you do, belongs in the same comparison.

FAQs about Aadhaar Verification API Pricing

How much does an Aadhaar verification API cost per transaction?

Costs vary by provider and by which verification steps you use. Hypersign charges 6 rupees per Aadhaar verification and 15 rupees for a full journey including face match, published openly on its pricing page. Most other providers, including HyperVerge and Signzy, require a sales call to get a number.

Is there a free Aadhaar verification API in India?

No major provider offers a fully free production tier for Aadhaar verification, since each check involves a real cost tied to UIDAI or Sub-AUA infrastructure. Several vendors, including Hypersign, Setu, and Surepass, offer a free sandbox so you can test integration before paying for live verifications.

What's included in Aadhaar verification API pricing besides the per-transaction fee?

Many providers bundle a monthly minimum commitment that covers a set number of verifications before overage charges apply. Hypersign's 7,500 rupee minimum covers 500 verification journeys, including OTP eKYC, offline QR, and face match, with consent management and data minimisation included in that price rather than billed separately.

Does an Aadhaar verification API help with DPDP Act compliance?

It can, but only if consent management and data minimisation are actually built into the product. Hypersign captures purpose-based consent with a signed audit trail and supports selective disclosure so you share only what a check requires, both included in the price, while providers without a published consent product typically leave that work to a separate tool or your own team.
