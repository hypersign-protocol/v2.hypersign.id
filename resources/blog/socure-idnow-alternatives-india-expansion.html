Socure & IDnow Alternatives for India Expansion (2026)

Socure's fraud-risk scoring and IDnow's supervised video-ident are both strong in their home markets, but neither publishes a single India-specific claim. Here's where each actually fits, and the one alternative built for the DPDP, CKYC, and Aadhaar gap both leave open.

Vikram Anand Bhushan, Co-founder & CPO, Hypersign·August 28, 2026·7 min read

Socure and IDnow solve different problems well. Socure's Sigma models are built for US fraud-risk decisioning, and IDnow's AutoIdent and VideoIdent are built for EU video-KYC. Both are named in Hypersign's own best identity verification providers roundup, and neither publishes a single India-specific claim anywhere on its site. A team already running on one of them that starts expanding into India, where DPDP consent rules, CKYC records, and Aadhaar-linked document formats all apply, ends up searching for socure alternatives or idnow alternatives for the same reason: the gap isn't a flaw in either product, it's a market neither was built to serve.

This post covers where Socure and IDnow actually fit, and the one alternative built specifically for the India-expansion gap both leave open, Hypersign, where our team works.

What to Look For in a Socure or IDnow Alternative for India Expansion

Before comparing vendors, it helps to know what actually separates a good fit from a bad one. Plenty of socure competitors and idnow competitors exist; few of them solve the specific India problem:

  • India-specific rail support, not a country count. A "190+ countries" or "600+ customers worldwide" claim says nothing about CKYC integration or Aadhaar-linked document formats.
  • DPDP compliant KYC, not GDPR language relabeled. Purpose-bound consent, an audit trail, and a right-to-erasure path are DPDP requirements, not GDPR ones, and most identity verification vendors don't ship any of that as a product feature.
  • Published pricing versus a sales quote. Whether a vendor puts a rate card on its site is a decent proxy for how easy it is to start with as a smaller team.
  • Fraud and compliance depth kept, not traded away. Adding India coverage shouldn't mean giving up the fraud-risk scoring or regulatory-grade video-ident either vendor already does well.
  • Reuse that isn't network-locked. A verified identity should be reusable across compliant partners, not just other customers of the same vendor.

Where Socure Actually Fits

Socure's own homepage positions it as "AI-Native Trust Infrastructure for Identity, Risk, Compliance, Age, and Workforce," and the numbers behind that claim are real. Sigma Identity Fraud runs on transformer-based models trained on real-time phone-porting data and cross-network intelligence from the Socure Identity Graph, and Socure says it holds manual review rates under 5%, against a 10 to 15% industry average. The company has raised over $742 million since its 2012 founding, including a $156 million round in 2025 at a $5.2 billion valuation, and it counts 19 of the top 20 US banks and 13 of the top 15 US credit card issuers as customers.

That customer list is the tell. Every named metric on Socure's site is US-labeled: US banks, US credit card issuers, US public-sector organizations, and Sigma's fraud signals lean on SSN verification and phone-porting data built for US identity infrastructure. Socure's own international-expansion announcements have named Canada, UK, and Europe, and a February 2026 bank-account-verification expansion added "more than 30 countries" without naming a single one. Nowhere on socure.com, in its product pages, or in its press coverage does India, Aadhaar, CKYC, or DPDP appear.

Pricing follows the same pattern. Socure doesn't publish a rate card, DevHub's own pricing details sit behind a dashboard login, so a team has to start a sales conversation before it sees a number, let alone one scoped to an India rollout.

None of that makes Socure a bad product. Its fraud-risk scoring and its penetration into US banking are real strengths, just not ones built with an India expansion in mind.

Where IDnow Actually Fits

IDnow's pitch is "Go Beyond KYC to TYC," Trust Your Customer, a platform that layers electronic-signature and trust services on top of standard identity checks. AutoIdent and VideoIdent are IDnow's real strength: a supervised, human-assisted video check that BaFin and other EU regulators require for specific onboarding flows, something automated document scanning alone can't satisfy. IDnow Trust Services AB became a certified Qualified Trust Service Provider in the EU in November 2024, and the company describes itself as one of Europe's first eIDAS 2.0 identity-verification providers.

Every regulatory reference on IDnow's site is German or EU-specific: BaFin, Germany's Money Laundering Act (GwG), the Telekommunikationsgesetz for telecom onboarding, and eIDAS Regulation (EU) No 910/2014. Support hours are quoted in CET, and the site's language options stop at German, French, and English. Founded in Munich in 2014 and still headquartered there, IDnow has never published an India-specific claim, an Aadhaar or CKYC integration, or any DPDP compliance language.

Pricing isn't published either. Every IDnow deal routes through a quote scoped to the specific regulatory obligation being met, the same sales-gated model Socure runs.

None of that makes IDnow a bad product. Its supervised video-identification and EU trust-services depth are genuine strengths few competitors match, just not ones aimed at a market outside Europe.

If a supervised video check for EU regulatory reasons is the requirement, IDnow's AutoIdent and VideoIdent, backed by its eIDAS 2.0 and QTSP certification, cover ground few competitors do.
