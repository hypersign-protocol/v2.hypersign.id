Sumsub Pricing in 2026: What It Actually Costs

Sumsub pricing publishes real numbers for two tiers, Basic and Compliance, then stops. Here's what Sumsub actually charges per verification, what sits behind the unpriced Enterprise tier, and how the total compares to Hypersign's flat rate at volume.

Vikram Anand Bhushan, Co-founder & CPO, Hypersign·August 27, 2026·7 min read

"Sumsub pricing" resolves the same way for most people who search it: two real numbers, followed by a wall of "Enterprise, let's talk." That's not unusual for this category, most identity verification vendors price the same way, but it means a team comparing Sumsub cost against alternatives is comparing a published rate card against an unpublished one for everything past the entry tiers.

This post lays out exactly what Sumsub's own pricing page publishes, what it doesn't, and what a team ends up paying at a representative volume, sourced from Sumsub's pricing page directly rather than a reseller's summary of it.

KYB in particular has no self-serve price anywhere on Sumsub's site. A team that needs business verification alongside individual KYC is routed to a sales conversation regardless of volume, even if every other check it runs fits comfortably inside Basic or Compliance.

Reading the Fine Print on Sumsub Cost

Both published tiers come with a 14-day free trial covering 50 checks. Past that, the billing rule is stated plainly on the pricing page: "the minimum amount that you pay each month. Once you exceed this amount, you will be charged for all additional checks," prorated on both Basic and Compliance, with charges applying only to successful verifications.

Sumsub ID, the free reuse feature included even on Basic, is worth a second look before treating it as a cost saver. It only helps when the other company checking your user is also a Sumsub customer, it's a distribution advantage tied to Sumsub's client list, not an open credential standard a user can carry to any verifier. Hypersign's Sumsub comparison covers this distinction in more depth.

A commonly cited figure puts Sumsub's AML screening add-on at $1.36 per check on top of the Compliance tier, but that number doesn't appear on Sumsub's current public pricing page text. Treat it as unconfirmed and check directly with Sumsub sales before budgeting against it, rather than as a freshly verified rate.

Which Pricing Model Actually Fits

Sumsub's Basic tier is a reasonable starting point if document and liveness checks are the whole requirement and AML, KYB, and fraud correlation aren't yet in scope. Its strengths outside pricing are real: coverage across 220-plus countries and 14,000-plus document types, a Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader standing for identity verification, and a dedicated FATF Travel Rule suite that's genuinely hard to match for crypto exchanges and VASPs already deep in that ecosystem.

  • Crypto or VASP compliance is the primary use case? Sumsub's Travel Rule tooling and exchange-network depth are a real edge worth the Compliance or Enterprise tier.
  • Need KYB, consent management, and reusable credentials self-serve, without an Enterprise sales cycle? That's the specific gap Hypersign's flat-rate model is built to close.
  • Need AML screening specifically? Confirm the current add-on rate directly with either vendor's sales team; neither publishes it as a flat, self-serve number.

FAQ

How much does Sumsub cost per verification?

Sumsub's Basic plan runs $1.35 per verification with a $149/month minimum. The Compliance plan, which adds AML screening and ongoing monitoring, runs $1.85 per verification with a $299/month minimum. Both figures come directly from Sumsub's own pricing page.

Is Sumsub's pricing public?

Partially. Sumsub publishes rates for Basic and Compliance, its two self-serve tiers. Everything past that, KYB, non-document verification, Fraud Detection & Prevention, full ongoing monitoring, and Travel Rule tooling, sits behind a custom Enterprise quote with no published number.

What isn't included in Sumsub's published pricing plans?

Business verification (KYB), non-document verification, database validation, cross-customer Fraud Detection & Prevention, and FATF Travel Rule tooling are all gated behind the unpriced Enterprise tier. None of them appear as a self-serve line item on Basic or Compliance.

How does Sumsub vs Hypersign pricing compare?

At 1,200 verifications a month, Sumsub's Basic tier runs roughly $1,620 and Compliance runs roughly $2,220, against Hypersign's flat $900 at the same volume, with consent management and a credential vault bundled into that rate rather than priced separately.
