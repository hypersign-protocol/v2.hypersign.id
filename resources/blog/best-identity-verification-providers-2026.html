7 Identity Verification Providers, Tested on Coverage, Speed & Price (2026)

We compared seven identity verification providers on document and country coverage, verification speed, fraud detection depth, and real per-check pricing, including the two that won't publish a rate card.

Someone on your team searches "identity verification providers," opens six tabs, and finds six vendors all claiming 99% accuracy and global coverage. None of the marketing pages say what a check actually costs, and half of them route you straight to a sales call before you find out. We put together this list to fix that: seven identity verification providers, including Hypersign, where our team works, compared on the things that actually decide whether one fits your product: document and country coverage, verification speed, fraud detection depth, and real per-check pricing.

What Identity Verification Providers Actually Do (and Don't)

An identity verification provider does one job: confirm that a document is real and that the person holding it is who the document says they are. That means reading the document through OCR, MRZ, and barcode data, checking it for signs of tampering, and often matching a live selfie against the photo on the document. Some providers add NFC chip reading for passports and electronic IDs, which checks a cryptographic signature against the issuing government's own public key instead of just looking at the printed page.

What it usually doesn't do is the rest of a compliance program. KYB checks on a business, AML and sanctions screening, and ongoing monitoring after onboarding are separate disciplines, and not every vendor on this list handles them. Some of the platforms below work more like identity verification software you configure yourself; others are closer to a managed service. If you need the fuller compliance stack, our guide to KYC providers covers that comparison instead. This one stays focused on identity verification itself, because most teams shopping for "identity verification providers" and most teams shopping for "KYC providers" are actually asking two different questions.

Buyers shopping for identity verification companies usually care about four things:

  • Document and country coverage. A provider that reads 20 document types well will struggle the moment your users show up with a national ID it has never seen. Coverage claims are easy to print and hard to verify, so ask for the actual document list, not just a country count.
  • Speed. Every extra second in a verification flow is a user who might not finish it. A slow OCR pass or a manual review queue that takes hours instead of seconds shows up directly in your conversion numbers.
  • Fraud detection depth. Template matching and OCR catch typos, not a printed photo held up to a webcam. Liveness detection, deepfake detection, and device or IP risk scoring are a different layer entirely, and a provider that skips them is only checking half the problem.
  • Pricing transparency. Whether a vendor publishes a rate card or routes you through a sales call says a lot about how easy they'll be to start with as a smaller team, and how long you'll wait to find out if a provider even fits your budget.

Top 7 Identity Verification Providers of 2026

These seven show up most often when teams evaluate identity verification companies, across a spread of pricing models: self-serve and published, usage-based, and fully sales-gated. We list Hypersign last, not because it belongs last, but because the fairest way to compare a vendor you work for is to make its own team explain it after everyone else has had a turn.

1. Veriff

Veriff is a self-serve identity verification platform built for teams that want to start verifying users today, not after a sales cycle. It's one of the more developer-friendly names on this list to get running quickly, and its entry-level pricing is public, which is unusual in this category.

Key Features

  • Automated document and biometric verification with real-time decisioning
  • Broad global document coverage
  • Self-serve signup with no sales call required to get started
  • API and no-code integration options
  • Real-time fraud and liveness checks built into the standard flow

Pricing

Veriff publishes self-serve pricing starting from $0.80 per verification, with no published monthly minimum on its entry tier. That makes it one of the few providers on this list you can actually price out before talking to anyone. Confirm the current rate on Veriff's site before you budget against it, since vendor pricing shifts often, and higher-volume or enterprise tiers move to custom quotes.

Real Use Cases

A marketplace launching in a new country can turn on Veriff and start verifying sellers the same week, without waiting on a procurement cycle. A startup testing product-market fit can verify its first thousand users without committing to an annual contract.

A small team without a dedicated compliance hire can also lean on Veriff's self-serve dashboard to handle manual review edge cases directly, instead of building an internal review queue from scratch.

2. Sumsub

Sumsub is one of the more recognized names in this space, especially among teams that need kyc identity verification and AML screening from a single vendor rather than two. It's grown a large footprint in crypto and Web3, where travel-rule and sanctions tooling matter as much as document checks.

Key Features

  • Document and biometric verification with liveness detection
  • Published tiered pricing, which is rare in this category
  • Strong crypto and Web3 customer base
  • Compliance add-ons available on higher tiers
  • Case management tooling for manual review teams

Pricing

The Basic plan starts at $149 a month minimum, the Compliance plan runs $299 a month, and per-check pricing sits around $1.85. Both numbers are worth confirming on Sumsub's own pricing page before you build a budget around them, since which features sit behind which tier can shift.

Real Use Cases

A crypto exchange that needs identity verification plus AML screening in one vendor can start on Sumsub's Basic plan and move up as compliance requirements grow. A fintech that outgrew a cheaper self-serve tool often lands here next, once ongoing monitoring becomes a real requirement rather than a nice-to-have.

3. Persona

Persona is a developer-first identity platform built around a flexible workflow builder rather than one fixed verification flow. Its documentation is a step above most of this list, and it's become popular well outside regulated fintech, including in fraud and agentic-AI use cases where a rigid, one-size-fits-all flow doesn't work.

Key Features

  • Highly configurable verification workflows and conditional logic
  • Strong API design and developer documentation
  • Multiple verification methods combinable in a single flow
  • Popular outside regulated fintech too, including fraud and agentic-AI use cases
  • Granular control over what data is collected and stored at each step

Pricing

Persona's pricing is usage-based and commonly cited in the $2 to $5 per check range depending on configuration, though the full rate card isn't public. Confirm current pricing directly with Persona before you commit to a plan, since the final number depends heavily on which verification methods you enable.

Real Use Cases

A product team that needs different verification steps for different user segments, a lighter flow for low-risk signups and a heavier one for high-value transactions, can build that logic natively instead of stitching together two vendors.

A platform verifying both humans and AI agents acting on their behalf, an increasingly common ask, can also configure Persona's workflows around that distinction rather than forcing every actor through the same document check.

4. Jumio

Jumio is one of the original players in this category, and it's still a common choice for large, regulated enterprises with an existing vendor security review process already built around names like it. That track record is the product as much as any single feature.

Key Features

  • Broad document and biometric verification suite
  • Enterprise-grade SLAs and dedicated support
  • Long track record in banking and financial services
  • Deep integration options for large-scale deployments
  • Established relationships with large regulated institutions

Pricing

Jumio doesn't publish a rate card. Third-party estimates put per-check costs somewhere between $3 and $5, with annual contracts typically starting around $50,000 and climbing past $200,000 for large enterprise deployments. Treat those figures as estimates, not quotes, until Jumio's sales team confirms a number for your actual volume.

Real Use Cases

A large bank that already runs Jumio through its vendor security review process, or one that needs a name-brand vendor to satisfy an internal procurement checklist, is the clearest fit here. The contract size and sales cycle rarely make sense for an early-stage team.

5. IDnow

IDnow is a European identity verification platform built for industries where a supervised video check is a regulatory requirement, not an option. It's one of the few vendors on this list with real, production-scale experience running human-assisted verification alongside the automated kind.

Key Features

  • NFC-based passport and eID chip reading under the ICAO 9303 standard
  • Human-assisted video identification (AutoIdent) for regulations that require it
  • Live across Europe, processing tens of thousands of verifications a month
  • Strong fit for EU banking, insurance, and telecom compliance requirements
  • Supervised and automated flows available under one platform

Pricing

IDnow doesn't publish pricing. You'll need to request a quote, which is typical for vendors selling into heavily regulated European markets, where pricing usually reflects the specific regulatory obligation being met rather than a flat per-check rate.

Real Use Cases

An EU bank or telecom that legally needs a supervised video-identification option, not just automated document checks, has few vendors that cover this ground as well as IDnow does. An insurer rolling out a new digital product in a country that mandates video-KYC for certain policy types faces the same constraint.

6. HyperVerge

HyperVerge is an India and APAC-focused identity verification platform with broad claimed document coverage across that region. It shows up frequently in its own right in "best identity verification providers" comparisons, which tells you where its go-to-market focus sits.

Key Features

  • Claimed coverage across 190-plus countries, with government-source verification in over 100
  • AI-driven document authentication and processing
  • Volume-based pricing tiers
  • Deep regional document depth in India and APAC markets
  • Free trial available before a full commercial commitment

Pricing

HyperVerge doesn't publish pricing publicly. Rates are quote-based and drop with volume, which you'll only see once you're in a sales conversation, though a free trial is available to test the product first.

Real Use Cases

A fintech or platform building specifically for India or the wider APAC region, where document formats and edge cases are HyperVerge's core strength, is the use case this vendor is built around.

7. Hypersign

Hypersign, where our team works, is a document verification API built around one question most vendors on this list don't ask: what happens after the check passes. Most of this category treats verification as a one-time event, checked once at signup and then forgotten. Hypersign treats it as something that has to stay valid.

Key Features

  • 14,000-plus document types across 189-plus countries, 130-plus languages, and 50-plus scripts
  • Visual zone OCR, MRZ, barcode, and NFC chip reading cross-validated against each other in one pass
  • NFC passport and eID reading verified against the issuing government's public key
  • 200-plus fraud signals, including template matching, hologram and microprint detection, portrait substitution, deepfake detection, and device and IP risk scoring
  • Sub-2-second verification, with a 1.42-second p95 backend response time and a 99.99% uptime SLA
  • Automatic document-expiry tracking for ongoing KYC, not just a one-time check

Pricing

Pricing is published, not sales-gated. The Starter plan runs $500 per quarter for the first year, with 100 checks included per quarter, then drops to $0.75 per check pay-as-you-go with no minimum from year two. There's a free sandbox to test the integration before any commercial conversation starts, which puts Hypersign in the small group on this list you can actually try before you buy.

Real Use Cases

A fintech or neobank can verify a new account holder in under 30 seconds without a branch visit. A crypto exchange can meet FATF Travel Rule, MiCA, and AMLD6 requirements at onboarding, with document coverage broad enough to handle a truly global user base.

An iGaming platform can age-gate registration before a user's first deposit, with document expiry tracked automatically afterward instead of left to quietly lapse until an audit catches it.

Pricing Overview

  • Veriff: from $0.80 per check, no published minimum, self-serve
  • Sumsub: $149 to $299 a month minimum, plus roughly $1.85 per check
  • Persona: roughly $2 to $5 per check, usage-based, partially published
  • Jumio: not published, estimated $3 to $5 per check, contracts from $50,000 a year
  • IDnow: not published, quote-based
  • HyperVerge: not published, quote-based, volume tiers, free trial available
  • Hypersign: $500 per quarter starter with 100 checks included, then $0.75 per check with no minimum from year two, free sandbox

Which Identity Verification Provider Should You Actually Pick

If you need a supervised video check for EU regulatory reasons, IDnow covers ground the others on this list don't. If you're building specifically for India or APAC, HyperVerge's regional document depth is hard to match. If your workflow needs conditional logic a fixed flow can't handle, Persona's flexibility is worth the higher per-check cost. And if a name-brand enterprise vendor with a well-trodden security review process matters more than price, Jumio is the safer procurement choice.

For most teams comparing identity verification providers on coverage, speed, and price without a sales call standing in the way, three names on this list actually publish their pricing: Veriff, Sumsub, and Hypersign. Between those three, Hypersign is built for what happens after the first check, with 189-plus country coverage, sub-2-second verification, and document-expiry tracking that keeps a verified user valid instead of quietly stale.
