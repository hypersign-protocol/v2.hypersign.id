Best Veriff Alternatives for Web3 & Crypto KYC (2026)

Generic "Veriff alternatives" lists skip what VASPs, DeFi platforms, and exchanges actually need: AML that isn't a stacked add-on, KYB priced up front, and credentials that travel across protocols. Here are 8 real alternatives, including Hypersign.

Vikram Anand Bhushan, Co-founder & CPO, Hypersign·August 27, 2026·9 min read

Search "veriff alternatives" and you get generic identity-verification roundups built for fintech and marketplace buyers. None of them address what a VASP, DeFi platform, or exchange actually needs: reusable credentials across protocols, an AML model that doesn't stack a surcharge on top of every check, and a data custody story that doesn't recreate the centralized-honeypot risk Web3 teams are trying to avoid in the first place. Here are the real Veriff competitors for crypto and Web3 KYC specifically, including Hypersign, where our team works.

Where Veriff Is Strong, and Where Web3 Teams Hit a Wall

To be fair to Veriff first: its biometric and liveness stack is built in-house, certified to ISO/IEC 30107-3 Level 1 and Level 2 for presentation-attack detection, and backed by a G2 Leader badge (Summer 2026, 4.5/5). It covers 230+ countries and territories and 12,500+ document types, and its self-serve pricing is genuinely public, a real strength in a category full of "contact sales" rate cards. Blockchain.com has run identity checks through Veriff since 2018, behind more than $1 trillion in crypto transactions, so the crypto-scale track record is real too.

The gap for Web3-specific buyers shows up in three places. AML and sanctions screening is a paid add-on on every Veriff tier (+$0.64 per verification), not included in the base rate exchanges and VASPs actually need for regulatory compliance. KYB only entered Veriff's product line through its February 2026 acquisition of KYB provider Vespia, with commercial availability targeted for mid-2026 and pricing still unpublished. And no travel-rule tooling, DID or W3C Verifiable Credential support, or self-custodial data model appears anywhere in Veriff's public product pages, verification data sits in Veriff's own systems on a 3-to-6-month default retention window, extendable to two years for a further add-on fee, rather than in a credential a user holds and can present elsewhere.

None of that makes Veriff a bad product. It makes it a general-purpose identity verification platform that a crypto or Web3 team can absolutely run on, just not one purpose-built around VASP obligations or credential reuse across protocols. That's the specific gap this list covers.

What to Look For in a Veriff Alternative for Crypto and Web3

A handful of criteria separate a good fit from a bad one when the buyer is a VASP, exchange, or DeFi protocol rather than a traditional fintech:

  • AML and travel-rule depth included, not stacked. Sanctions screening, PEP checks, and travel-rule data exchange are core VASP obligations, not optional add-ons priced separately from the base verification rate.
  • Data custody model. Does the vendor store raw documents in a database it controls, or issue a credential the user holds? This affects breach exposure directly, not just alignment with decentralization as a philosophy.
  • Reusable, portable credentials. Can a user verified on one protocol present that status to another without resubmitting documents, or is every integration a fresh silo?
  • On-chain and DID support. Does the vendor issue credentials against open W3C standards, or is verification status locked inside a proprietary, off-chain database?
  • KYB priced up front. Business verification for exchange counterparties and institutional onboarding should have a published rate, not a sales call.

8 Best Veriff Alternatives for Web3 & Crypto KYC

These eight cover the range: general-purpose platforms with genuine crypto traction, Web3-native specialists, and Hypersign. We list Hypersign first, since it's the one purpose-built around the exact gap this list is scoped to, but every entry below is a real option worth evaluating against your own jurisdictions and volume.

1. Hypersign

Hypersign runs regulatory-grade KYC, KYB, and AML through the same pipeline as Veriff, document capture, biometric matching, liveness and deepfake detection, sanctions screening, but issues the result as a W3C-standard verifiable credential to a user-controlled encrypted vault instead of a record in a centralized database. A user verified once can present that credential to any other Hypersign-integrated protocol without resubmitting documents, the model already running in production for on-chain KYC on Nibiru Chain.

Key Features

  • Document OCR/MRZ across 189+ countries and 14,000+ document types, plus native active and passive biometric liveness with face match
  • KYB with UBO and director checks at $20/business, included at every paid tier, not gated behind a sales quote
  • AML screening across 1,300+ watchlists, self-serve add-on with no monthly minimum to clear first
  • Consent API and Encrypted Identity Vault included from the Starter plan
  • W3C DID Core 1.0 (did:hid) and selective-disclosure verifiable credentials, so a verifier sees an over-18 flag instead of a birthdate

Pricing

The Starter plan runs $500 per quarter for the first year, then drops to $0.75 per verification with no monthly minimum from year two, AML, KYB, and consent included in that structure rather than metered as separate line items. See the full Hypersign vs Veriff comparison for a feature-by-feature breakdown against Veriff's exact tiers.

Real Use Cases

A VASP that needs AML and KYB priced up front, not scoped through an Enterprise sales call, can run both from the Starter plan on day one. A DeFi protocol that wants a user's verified status to travel to a second, third, or fourth integration without re-collecting documents each time is exactly what the DID/VC layer is built for.

2. Sumsub

Sumsub is one of the most established names in crypto-focused KYC, widely used by exchanges and VASPs and recognized for its AML and travel-rule capabilities.

Key Features

  • Advanced Liveness detection with 3D face mapping and face match
  • Deep AML screening and travel-rule tooling built for exchange-scale compliance
  • Published tiered pricing, unusual for a vendor this deep into enterprise compliance

Pricing

The Basic plan runs $1.35 per verification with a $149 a month minimum (ID verification plus liveness and face match). Compliance runs $1.85 per verification with a $299 a month minimum and adds AML screening. A 14-day free trial includes 50 free checks.

Real Use Cases

An exchange that needs sanctions screening and travel-rule data exchange at real transaction volume is Sumsub's strongest fit. Where it's weaker for smaller Web3 teams: KYB, full AML tooling, and credential reuse tend to sit behind a custom Enterprise tier rather than self-serve pricing.

3. Persona

Persona is a well-funded, developer-first identity platform with a flexible, modular workflow builder popular well beyond Web3.

Key Features

  • Modular, no-code workflow builder for custom verification flows
  • Strong API design and documentation
  • Recent expansion into agentic-AI and fraud use cases

Pricing

Persona doesn't publish a flat rate card, pricing is usage-based and scoped through a sales conversation once volume is established.

Real Use Cases

A team that wants to build a highly custom verification flow, and has engineering bandwidth to configure it, gets the most out of Persona's builder. Consent management and a user-owned credential vault aren't part of the core product, a gap for teams that want verified status reused rather than re-collected per integration.

4. Fractal ID

Fractal ID was among the first identity platforms built specifically for Web3 clients, serving protocols like Polygon ID, Ripple, and Avalanche.

Key Features

  • KYC flows designed around DeFi and DAO onboarding specifically
  • Early, genuine Web3-native positioning rather than a retrofit

Pricing

Pricing isn't published; teams request access and pricing directly.

Real Use Cases

Fractal ID's July 2024 breach, which exposed roughly 50,000 users' KYC documents in just over two hours (covered in our earlier analysis), is a useful case study in the risk of storing raw identity documents in a single centralized vault, regardless of how Web3-aligned the front-end product is.

5. Civic

Civic is one of the longest-running Web3 identity projects, known for its Solana-based proof-of-personhood and reusable "Civic Pass" credential.

Key Features

  • Reusable, on-chain "Civic Pass" credential
  • Lightweight proof-of-personhood and age-check flows

Pricing

Pricing is usage-based and scoped per integration; check current rates directly with Civic.

Real Use Cases

A DAO or protocol that primarily needs lightweight, on-chain personhood or age verification, rather than full regulatory KYC/KYB, fits Civic well. Teams that need deep AML screening or enterprise compliance workflows alongside identity checks will typically need to pair Civic with another vendor.

6. HyperVerge

HyperVerge is an eKYC and video-KYC specialist with patented liveness detection and particularly strong coverage across India, Southeast Asia, and Africa, markets many Web3 platforms are actively trying to reach.

Key Features

  • Patented liveness detection
  • Strong document and biometric coverage in South Asia, Southeast Asia, and Africa

Pricing

Pricing is quote-based; contact HyperVerge directly for current rates.

Real Use Cases

A Web3 platform onboarding heavily in the regions HyperVerge specializes in gets stronger document and biometric depth there than most general-purpose vendors. AML depth and credential reuse across platforms are less developed than its document-verification core.

7. AU10TIX

AU10TIX is one of the oldest players in the identity verification space, with an enterprise customer base that includes large marketplaces and telcos.

Key Features

  • Document-forgery detection built on a long history of fraud pattern data
  • Enterprise-grade deployment for high-volume, high-fraud-risk onboarding

Pricing

Pricing isn't published; AU10TIX is built primarily around Enterprise sales conversations.

Real Use Cases

A large exchange with high fraud exposure and the procurement bandwidth for an enterprise sales cycle is the clearest fit. Smaller Web3 teams typically find the packaging and sales process heavier than a self-serve API.

8. iDenfy

iDenfy has built a reputation for affordable, fast-to-deploy KYC, and is a common pick for early-stage startups, including Web3 projects, that need a compliant flow live quickly on a small budget.

Key Features

  • Fast onboarding and setup
  • Solid core document and biometric checks at a lower entry cost

Pricing

iDenfy publishes entry-level rates aimed at smaller teams; confirm current pricing directly, as tiers shift.

Real Use Cases

An early-stage Web3 project that needs a compliant verification flow live fast, without a large budget, is iDenfy's core use case. Reusable credentials and on-chain issuance aren't part of its model, it's a traditional, centralized verification-as-a-service product.

Pricing Overview

  • Veriff: $0.80 to $1.89 per verification, $49 to $209 a month minimum by tier, AML add-on +$0.64/verification, KYB unpriced
  • Hypersign: $500 per quarter starter for year one, then $0.75 per verification with no minimum, AML/KYB/consent included in that structure
  • Sumsub: $1.35 to $1.85 per verification, $149 to $299 a month minimum by tier
  • Persona: usage-based, sales-scoped, not published
  • Fractal ID: not published, request access directly
  • Civic: usage-based per integration, not published
  • HyperVerge: quote-only
  • AU10TIX: not published, Enterprise sales cycle
  • iDenfy: published entry-level rates, confirm current tiers directly

Choosing a Veriff Alternative for Your Crypto or Web3 Team

If deep AML and travel-rule tooling for a high-volume exchange is the priority, Sumsub's maturity is hard to beat. If the need is lightweight, Web3-native proof-of-personhood, Civic or Fractal ID are purpose-built for that, with Fractal ID's 2024 breach worth weighing against its Web3-native positioning. If regional document and liveness coverage in South or Southeast Asia matters most, HyperVerge has the deepest local expertise on this list. If budget and speed to launch are the constraint, iDenfy is a reasonable starting point.

For a VASP, exchange, or DeFi platform that needs the full regulatory picture, KYC, KYB, and AML, without a monthly minimum or a stacked AML surcharge, and wants verified users to carry a reusable, self-custodial credential across every protocol they touch rather than re-verifying at each one, Hypersign is the option built specifically for that combination.
