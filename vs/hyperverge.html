Choosing Between Them

Neither Is Wrong. They Fit Different Teams.

The real gap isn't pricing, it's who owns your PII liability.

Treating verification as a one-time transaction means every document, selfie, and match score gets re-collected and re-stored on every platform a user touches. Hypersign treats identity as reusable instead, consent, encryption, and portable credentials mean the same verified identity gets reused, not re-collected, which is what actually reduces your liability. GDPR, DPDP, and similar frameworks already require purpose-bound consent, an audit trail, and a right-to-erasure path. Most KYC vendors, HyperVerge included, don't ship any of that as part of the verification product, so you either go without it or bolt on a separate consent vendor, a second integration, SLA, and bill.
