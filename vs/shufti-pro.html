Choosing Between Them

Neither Is Wrong. They Fit Different Teams.

The real gap isn't $0.75 vs $0.95, it's who owns your PII liability.

Treating verification as a one-time transaction means every document, selfie, and match score gets re-collected and re-stored on every platform a user touches. Hypersign treats identity as reusable instead, consent, encryption, and portable credentials mean the same verified identity gets reused, not re-collected, which is what actually reduces your liability. GDPR, DPDP, and similar frameworks already require purpose-bound consent, an audit trail, and a right-to-erasure path. Most KYC vendors, Shufti Pro included, don't ship any of that as part of the verification product, so you either go without it or bolt on a separate consent vendor, a second integration, SLA, and bill.

Choose Shufti Pro if

  • You want the industry's rare, genuinely public pay-as-you-go rate for document and biometric checks with no sales call to get started.
  • iBeta Level 3-certified liveness and a broad certification stack (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, Cyber Essentials Plus) top your procurement checklist.
  • You're comfortable scoping AML, KYB, and video-ident through a separate Enterprise conversation once your volume grows.
  • You're comfortable owning consent capture, audit-trail, and revocation tooling yourself, or licensing it from a separate vendor, since none of it is part of Shufti's product.

Choose Hypersign if

  • You want to structurally reduce PII liability, consent, an immutable audit trail, and revocation live in the same verification flow, not a second vendor you have to onboard.
  • You want KYB and consent included at every paid tier, plus a user-owned vault, and AML available self-serve, no enterprise upsell required for any of it.
  • You want portable credentials built on open W3C DID/VC standards, so a verified user isn't re-checked, and their data isn't re-collected, on every platform they touch.
  • You want one contract covering verification plus the infrastructure regulators actually ask about, not a rate card, a future sales call, and a separate compliance vendor.
