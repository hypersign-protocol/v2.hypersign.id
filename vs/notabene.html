Notabene is the industry-leading FATF Travel Rule network for exchanging originator and beneficiary data between VASPs, it isn't a document or biometric KYC vendor. Hypersign verifies and onboards individual users and businesses onto crypto platforms; Notabene handles the counterparty messaging once a transaction crosses to another VASP. Most serious crypto compliance stacks eventually need both, not one instead of the other. Here's how the two actually fit together.

What Notabene Actually Is

The Travel Rule network for VASP-to-VASP transactions, not a KYC vendor.

Notabene's core product, SafeTransact, lets regulated crypto businesses identify a transaction counterparty, exchange encrypted originator and beneficiary data, and apply jurisdiction-specific risk rules before a transfer settles, the pre-transaction check FATF's Travel Rule (Recommendation 16) requires. It connects a claimed 2,000+ VASPs across 100+ jurisdictions via SafeGateway and its open TAP messaging standard, and is SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and Singapore PDPA compliant.

What it doesn't ship: document OCR, biometric liveness, KYB/UBO workflows, or consent-management and credential-vault infrastructure for the users and businesses being onboarded in the first place. Its "counterparty sanctions screening" runs through integrated AML providers, not a built-in watchlist engine, Notabene's job starts after a VASP already knows who its own customer is.

Choosing Between Them

This Usually Isn't Either/Or. It's Which One First.

The real question isn't Hypersign vs Notabene, it's whether your stack covers onboarding and inter-VASP transfer, or only one of the two.

A crypto platform's KYC obligation and its Travel Rule obligation are legally distinct events. Onboarding requires verifying who a customer is, document, biometric, KYB, sanctions screening, purpose-bound consent, before they ever move funds. The Travel Rule requires exchanging that customer's originator/beneficiary data with a counterparty VASP once a transaction crosses platforms, a separate FATF Recommendation 16 obligation Notabene specializes in. Neither vendor substitutes for the other; a platform with only Notabene still needs a KYC/KYB/AML system to know who its own customers are in the first place, and a platform with only Hypersign still needs a Travel Rule solution once it starts sending funds to other VASPs.

Hypersign's consent, audit-trail, and reusable-credential layer covers the individual onboarding side of that liability; Notabene's network covers the counterparty-messaging side. For how the two obligations map onto FATF and MiCA specifically, see What Is a VASP? and the MiCA deadline breakdown.

Choose Notabene if

  • You're already a regulated VASP, exchange, custodian, or payment provider that needs to exchange originator/beneficiary data with counterparty platforms under FATF's Travel Rule.
  • Network reach matters, you want access to a large, protocol-agnostic pool of 2,000+ already-connected VASPs rather than negotiating bilateral integrations.
  • You already have a working KYC/KYB/AML onboarding system and specifically need the pre-transaction counterparty layer on top of it.
  • You're comfortable with a free tier capped at $10k/month in outbound volume, then an Enterprise sales conversation for anything beyond it.

Choose Hypersign if

  • You need to actually verify who your users or business customers are, document, biometric, KYB, and AML, before any transaction, Travel Rule-eligible or not, happens.
  • You want consent capture, an audit trail, and a user-owned identity vault included as part of onboarding, not a separate compliance vendor to bolt on.
  • You want that onboarding to produce reusable, standards-based W3C credentials, useful groundwork if MiCA-style inter-CASP or multi-jurisdiction requirements are on your roadmap.
  • You want one contract covering document, biometric, KYB, and AML verification, live in days on a hosted API, widget, or magic link.
