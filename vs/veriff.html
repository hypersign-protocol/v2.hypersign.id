Choosing Between Them

Neither Is Wrong. They Fit Different Teams.

The real gap isn't $0.80 vs $0.75, it's who owns your PII liability.

Treating verification as a one-time transaction means every document, selfie, and match score gets re-collected and re-stored on every platform a user touches. Hypersign treats identity as reusable instead, consent, encryption, and portable credentials mean the same verified identity gets reused, not re-collected, which is what actually reduces your liability. GDPR, DPDP, and similar frameworks already require purpose-bound consent, an audit trail, and a right-to-erasure path. Most identity verification vendors, Veriff included, don't ship standalone consent or vault infrastructure as part of the verification product, so you either go without it or bolt on a separate consent vendor, a second integration, SLA, and bill.

Hypersign's $0.75 rate already includes this privacy layer; Veriff's $0.80–$1.89 range doesn't, and stacks a further +$0.64 for AML on top. Vendors that assemble similar depth from multiple APIs cost more still, see Hypersign vs Sumsub for a worked example.

  • You're comfortable with a self-serve rate card that carries a monthly minimum ($49–$209 depending on tier), rather than a pure pay-as-you-go model with no floor.
  • You're comfortable paying AML/PEP/sanctions screening as a separate +$0.64/verification add-on, and scoping KYB and Enterprise volume through a sales conversation.

  • You want KYB and consent included at every paid tier, plus a user-owned vault, and AML available self-serve with no monthly minimum to clear first.
  • You want portable credentials built on open W3C DID/VC standards, so a verified user isn't re-checked, and their data isn't re-collected, on every platform they touch.
  • You want one contract covering verification plus the infrastructure regulators actually ask about, not a rate card with per-tier floors, a stacked AML add-on, and a future sales call for KYB.
